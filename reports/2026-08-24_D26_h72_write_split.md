## 3e — Why H57 Phase 3 did not cover these

The H57 Phase 3 commit message (`59d356d`, 2026-08-19) is explicit. Its scope
was the **MAX TOOL LAYER**: `tool_executor._file_read`, `tool_executor._file_write`,
`tool_executor._shell_execute` (cwd resolution), `system_prompt.py` (git log cwd),
`quotes.py` (uploads_dir and generated_path for the quote flow output), and
`openclaw_worker.py` (drawings dir). H57 also converged two validators
(`tool_safety.validate_path` and the drawing-output `canonical_path`) into a
single canonical resolver, and removed `~/empire-repo/` from
`tool_safety.ALLOWED_ROOTS`. The commit's own stated rationale for the deferred
items was: *"not MAX read path, not writing client data"* — that rationale was
applied narrowly to six specific files (`self_heal.py`, `maintenance_manager.py`,
`code_task_runner.py`, `codeforge_desk.py`, `recovery.py`, `apostapp*.py`).

The writers in §3d are a different class: **backend service internals that
persist their own state via raw `open()` to a path resolved at module load
time**. They are not invoked through the MAX tool layer; they are scheduled by
the service loop (`desk_scheduler.py`) or invoked directly by routers. H57's
fix only intercepts paths that pass through `_file_read` / `_file_write` /
shell `cwd`. A direct `open(path, "w")` in a service routine never enters
that interception surface. The deferral was therefore a scope choice — not a
discovery. The commit message's enumeration of "deferred" writers was
undercounted: it listed six files, but the actual class of writers exceeds
thirty across `app/api`, `app/routers`, `app/services`, and `app/db`.

This explains why D23 (2026-08-24) found 348 files written under
`~/empire-repo/backend/data/` in 30 days despite H57's claimed closure:
**H57 closed the MAX-tool-layer leak. It did not close the backend-service-
internal leak.** The two surfaces are independent. A test that exercises
`tool_executor._file_write` cannot see a writer in `desk_scheduler._generate_morning_brief`.

Whether this is a defect (and therefore whether H57's claim "this class is
closed" was correct) depends on whether the canonical-root resolver should
be invoked by every backend writer, not just MAX-tool writers. **That question
is out of scope for this diagnostic.** The diagnosis says only: the class is
larger than H57 recorded; the brief is not frozen; the writer-reader path
hypothesis is disproved.
