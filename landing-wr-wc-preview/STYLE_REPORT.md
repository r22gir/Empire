# Empire Workroom — style report

Preview only. Not a publish recommendation until inbox routing is proven. Read with the three pages open: `a/`, `b/`, `c/`.

## What failed on the current page

The copy is already a workroom. The page is not. It uses a software-landing grammar: a cream field, mustard eyebrows, pill buttons, two equal cards, and a uniform three-up grid with tags painted on the photographs. Installs are thumbnails under the headline instead of the reason to stay. A designer who lives in Architectural Digest, Elle Decor, or Galerie reads that as a vendor site, not a shop that finishes rooms. EmpireBox is rightly quiet in the copy; the layout still feels like a product page.

## How the three styles differ

**A — Editorial atelier.** A masthead, a full-bleed cover, then the headline set like a feature opener. Installs are chapters (drapery, banquettes, beds, seating, WoodCraft last) with captions under the picture. Type is Newsreader + a plain sans. Palette is paper and ink; the only color accent is a dark umber, not mustard.

**B — Quiet luxury showroom.** The first screen is one room, edge to edge, with a short line of type on a scrim. The rest is a dark gallery: a cinematic plate, a sideways viewing room, and matted frames. Gold is a hairline and a label, not a button fill. Cormorant and a thin grotesque.

**C — Soft modern craft.** Airy paper with a light cloth texture. The first photograph carries a paper card for the headline so type never fights the room. The portfolio is a masonry of mixed frames plus one wide cinematic break. Fraunces, warm clay links, captions below the image.

All three keep the Workroom voice, put WoodCraft second, tuck EmpireBox into an optional disclosure, use only the 19 consent-yes heroes, and send mail to `workroom@empirebox.store`. No form. No ApostApp. No module grid.

## Recommendation

**Develop A.** Designers already trust editorial photography as the language of the rooms they specify. A puts a finished room before the pitch and gives the shop a table of contents instead of a catalog. B is the strongest mood and the right look for a single campaign still or an evening post; as the whole site it asks people to read long copy in a dark room and hides WoodCraft’s secondary role inside the same black field. C is the warmest and the most forgiving on a phone, and it is the fallback if A feels too much like a magazine and not enough like a workroom. If one site has to be chosen now, start from A and borrow C’s under-image captions if the print captions feel precious.

## Risks

- **Photographs.** The JPEGs are not in this git checkout. Empty frames say so until `assets/portfolio/` is filled from the existing preview folder. Judge the styles after that copy.
- **Weight.** Nineteen full-size camera files will be slow. Export about 1600px on the long edge before any host. Only the first frame loads eagerly.
- **Mobile.** A stacks to one column. B’s filmstrip has arrow buttons and keyboard arrows; the dark field is harder in sun. C’s masonry becomes one column on a phone.
- **Contrast.** Body type is ink on paper (A, C) or ivory on near-black (B). Umber and gold are for labels and links, checked against those backgrounds. No white-on-mustard buttons.
- **Motion.** B scrolls sideways. Reduced-motion makes that jump instant.
