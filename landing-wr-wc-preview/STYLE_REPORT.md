# Empire Workroom — style report

Preview only. Not a publish recommendation until inbox routing is proven. Read with the three pages open: `a/`, `b/`, `c/`.

## What failed on the current page

The live page uses a software-landing grammar: a cream field, mustard eyebrows, pill buttons, two equal cards, and a uniform three-up grid with tags painted on the photographs. Finished rooms are thumbnails under the headline instead of the reason to stay. A designer who lives in Architectural Digest, Elle Decor, or Galerie reads that as a vendor site, not a shop that makes beautiful soft goods. EmpireBox is rightly quiet in the copy; the layout still feels like a product page. The first draft of these previews fixed the layout and still sold the install as the whole product. The voice section below is the correction.

## How the three styles differ

**A — Editorial atelier.** A masthead, a full-bleed cover, then the headline set like a feature opener. Finished rooms are chapters (drapery, banquettes, beds, seating, WoodCraft last) with captions under the picture. Type is Newsreader + a plain sans. Palette is paper and ink; the only color accent is a dark umber, not mustard.

**B — Quiet luxury showroom.** The first screen is one room, edge to edge, with a short line of type on a scrim. The rest is a dark gallery: a cinematic plate, a sideways viewing room, and matted frames. Gold is a hairline and a label, not a button fill. Cormorant and a thin grotesque.

**C — Soft modern craft.** Airy paper with a light cloth texture. The first photograph carries a paper card for the headline so type never fights the room. The portfolio is a masonry of mixed frames plus one wide cinematic break. Fraunces, warm clay links, captions below the image.

All three put WoodCraft second, tuck EmpireBox into an optional disclosure, use only the 19 consent-yes heroes, and send mail to `workroom@empirebox.store`. No form. No ApostApp. No module grid. Shared offer lines now match. A is where the voice is fully set (mast, chapter ledes, close).

## Voice (founder, locked)

Lead with beauty: the rooms and soft goods a designer can have made. The line is **Your designs come true.** The designer envisions. Workroom produces and finishes so the vision lands. Photographs of finished rooms stay in the gallery as proof of that beauty. They are not the headline, and they are not the only thing the shop is.

Soft help, stated narrowly. On the quote, the shop can include **idea drawings / idea sheets**. Internally those sheets are parametric diagrams of the piece. On the page they are sheets that show the idea, sent with the quote. They are not construction documents. The page does not offer an interior design studio, a moodboard product, or a service that designs the whole project. Drawing Studio stays off the public page — it is shop and founder tooling. Photo Analyzer and Max are not named. EmpireBox stays in the disclosure.

Style A carries this in the mast (“Rooms & soft goods”), the headline, the deck, the chapter ledes, the four steps, and the close (“Bring us what you see”). B and C keep their layouts. Sentences they shared with A — the old “install is the design” headline, “working drawings,” “see recent installs,” “bring us the room” — were aligned so the three previews do not argue. Judge the words on A.

## Recommendation

**Develop A, with this voice.** Designers already trust editorial photography as the language of the rooms they specify. A puts a finished room before the pitch and gives the shop a table of contents instead of a catalog. The headline is now the promise, not the install. B is the strongest mood and the right look for a single campaign still or an evening post; as the whole site it asks people to read long copy in a dark room and hides WoodCraft’s secondary role inside the same black field. C is the warmest and the most forgiving on a phone, and it is the fallback if A feels too much like a magazine and not enough like a workroom. If one site has to be chosen now, start from A and borrow C’s under-image captions if the print captions feel precious.

## Risks

- **Overclaiming design help.** “Your designs come true” can be heard as “we design it for you.” The deck, the quote band, and step 02 keep authorship with the designer: you envision, your design stays yours, idea sheets show the piece. A later edit that adds moodboards, space planning, “design services,” or Drawing Studio as a client tool would overclaim. Trade readers may still hear “idea drawings” as shop drawings or construction documents. If that reading shows up, keep the public noun as idea sheets and keep them attached to the quote. Do not answer by describing a fuller drawing product.
- **Install creep.** Chapter titles are still the categories of finished work — drapery, banquettes, beds, seating, WoodCraft. That is the gallery, and it should stay. Putting “install” back into the headline, the mast, or the close returns the old pitch. Finishing the piece in the room remains the last step of a job, told once, not the promise.
- **Photographs.** The JPEGs are not in this git checkout. Empty frames say so until `assets/portfolio/` is filled from the existing preview folder. Judge the styles after that copy.
- **Weight.** Nineteen full-size camera files will be slow. Export about 1600px on the long edge before any host. Only the first frame loads eagerly.
- **Mobile.** A stacks to one column. B’s filmstrip has arrow buttons and keyboard arrows; the dark field is harder in sun. C’s masonry becomes one column on a phone.
- **Contrast.** Body type is ink on paper (A, C) or ivory on near-black (B). Umber and gold are for labels and links, checked against those backgrounds. No white-on-mustard buttons.
- **Motion.** B scrolls sideways. Reduced-motion makes that jump instant.
